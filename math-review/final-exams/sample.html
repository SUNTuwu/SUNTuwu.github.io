(c) 1
(d) 2

▶ Show Answer & Solution
**Answer: B** $$ \begin{gathered} \int_{0}^{1} 4 \cos (2 \pi x) \sin ^{2}(\pi x) d x=\int_{0}^{1} 2 \cos (2 \pi x)(1-\cos (2 \pi x)) d x \\ =\int_{0}^{1}(2 \cos (2 \pi x)-1-\cos (4 \pi x)) d x=\left[\frac{1}{\pi} \sin (2 \pi x)-x-\frac{1}{4 \pi} \sin (4 \pi x)\right]_{0}^{1}=-1 \end{gathered} $$

▶ Show Answer & Solution
(a) Divergent; by the Divergence Test since $$ \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{(-1)^{n} n}{\ln n} \text { does not exist } $$ (b) Divergent; by applying the Limit Comparison Test with respect to the divergent $p$-series $\sum \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ : [3 pts] $$ \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n^{2}+1}}}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}=\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \sqrt{\frac{n(n+2)}{n^{2}+1}}=1 $$ (c) Convergent; by applying the Root Test: $$ \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty}\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^{n^{2}}=\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} e^{n^{2} \ln \frac{n}{n+1}}=0<1 $$ since $$ \begin{aligned} & \lim _{x \rightarrow \infty} x^{2} \ln \frac{x}{x+1}=\lim _{x \rightarrow \infty} \frac{\ln x-\ln (x+1)}{x^{-2}} \\ & =\lim _{x \rightarrow \infty} \frac{\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{x+1}}{-2 x^{-3}}=\lim _{x \rightarrow \infty} \frac{-2 x^{3}}{x(x+1)}=-\infty \end{aligned} $$ (d) Convergent, since it is absolutely convergent $$ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|e^{-n} \sin n\right|<\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} e^{-n}=\frac{e^{-1}}{1-e^{-1}}<\infty $$

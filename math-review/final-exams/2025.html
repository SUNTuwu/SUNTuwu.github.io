Q18 [12 pts] Long
series-convergence alternating-series comparison-test
Determine whether the given series is convergent or divergent. Give reason to justify your answer for full credit. (a) $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2^{n}+5^{n}}{3^{n}+4^{n}}$. [4 pts] (b) $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1} \frac{\sqrt{n}+1}{n+1}$ [4 pts] (c) $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sqrt[3]{n^{3}+1}-n\right)$ [4 pts]
▶ Show Answer & Solution
(a) Divergent by the comparison test: since $\frac{2^{n}+5^{n}}{3^{n}+4^{n}}>\frac{5^{n}}{2 \cdot 4^{n}}>\frac{1}{2}$, and $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2}$ is a divergent series. Or by divergence test: $\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{2^{n}+5^{n}}{3^{n}+4^{n}}=\infty \neq 0$. (b) Convergent by the alternating series test: $$ \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{\sqrt{n}+1}{n+1}=0 $$ where $\frac{\sqrt{n}+1}{n+1}$ is a decreasing sequence since $$ \begin{aligned} & \frac{d}{d x} \frac{\sqrt{x}+1}{x+1}=\frac{(x+1) \frac{1}{2 \sqrt{x}}-(\sqrt{x}+1)}{(x+1)^{2}} \\ & \quad=\frac{1-x-2 \sqrt{x}}{2 \sqrt{x}(x+1)^{2}}<0 \text { for } x \geq 1 \end{aligned} $$ (c) Convergent by the comparison test: $$ \sqrt[3]{n^{3}+1}-n=\frac{n^{3}+1-n^{3}}{\left(n^{3}+1\right)^{2 / 3}+\left(n^{3}+1\right)^{1 / 3} n^{1 / 3}+n^{2 / 3}}<\frac{1}{n^{2}} $$ where $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^{2}}$ is a convergent series. Or use the limit comparison test by computing the following with a suitable choice of $p$ : $$ \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{\sqrt[3]{n^{3}+1}-n}{\frac{1}{n^{p}}}=\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{n\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{3}}\right)^{1 / 3}-1\right)}{\frac{1}{n^{p}}} $$

Q16 [10 pts] Long

The area under the graph of the function $y=x^{2} e^{-x^{2}}$ over the interval $0 \leq x<\infty$ is rotated about the $y$-axis to generated a solid of revolution. (a) Find the volume of this solid. ![](https://cdn.mathpix.com/cropped/cdc24773-e576-4579-9c15-258fed398d12-07.jpg?height=606&width=640&top_left_y=470&top_left_x=1242) (b) Suppose that the area is rotated about the $x$-axis to generate another solid of revolution. The area under the graph of the function $y=x^{2} e^{-x^{2}}$ over the interval $0 \leq x<\infty$ is rotated about the $y$-axis to generated a solid of revolution.
(a) Find the volume of this solid.
![](https://cdn.mathpix.com/cropped/cdc24773-e576-4579-9c15-258fed398d12-07.jpg?height=606&width=640&top_left_y=470&top_left_x=1242)
(b) Suppose that the area is rotated about the $x$-axis to generate another solid of revolution. Explain briefly why the volume of the solid thus obtained must be a finite value.
▶ Show Answer & Solution
(a) The volume of the solid is given by the improper integral $$ \begin{gathered} V=\int_{0}^{\infty} 2 \pi x \cdot x^{2} e^{-x^{2}} d x=\pi \int_{0}^{\infty}-x^{2} d e^{-x^{2}}=\pi\left[-\left.x^{2} e^{-x^{2}}\right|_{0} ^{\infty}+\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x^{2}} d x^{2}\right] \\ =0-\left.\pi e^{-x^{2}}\right|_{0} ^{\infty}=\pi \end{gathered} $$ (b) The volume of the solid is given by the improper integral $\int_{0}^{\infty} \pi x^{4} e^{-2 x^{2}} d x$. Note that $$ e^{2 x^{2}}=1+2 x^{2}+\frac{4 x^{4}}{2}+\frac{8 x^{6}}{3!}+\cdots>\frac{4 x^{6}}{3} $$ i.e., $x^{4} e^{-2 x^{2}}<\frac{3}{4 x^{2}}$. Since $\int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^{2}} d x$ is convergent, the volume of the solid is finite.

Find the limit of the infinite sequence. (a) $\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \cos \frac{n \pi}{2 n+1}$. (No partial credit.) (b) $\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{\ln n}{\ln (3 n)}$. (No partial credit.) (c) $\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} a_{n}$, where $a_{n}$ is an increasing and bounded sequence defined recursively by $$ a_{1}=2, \quad a_{n+1}=6-\frac{4}{a_{n}} . $$
Find the limit of the infinite sequence.
(a) $\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \cos \frac{n \pi}{2 n+1}$. (No partial credit.)
(b) $\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{\ln n}{\ln (3 n)}$. (No partial credit.)
(c) $\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} a_{n}$, where $a_{n}$ is an increasing and bounded sequence defined recursively by

$$
a_{1}=2, \quad a_{n+1}=6-\frac{4}{a_{n}} .
$$
▶ Show Answer & Solution
(a) $$ \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \cos \frac{n \pi}{2 n+1}=\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \cos \frac{\pi}{2+1 / n}=\cos \frac{\pi}{2}=0 $$ (b) $$ \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{\ln n}{\ln (3 n)}=\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{\ln n}{\ln 3+\ln n}=\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{1}{1+\ln 3 / \ln n}=1 $$ (c) Taking limits, we have $$ \begin{gathered} L=\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} a_{n+1}=6-\frac{4}{\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} a_{n}}=6-\frac{4}{L} \\ L^{2}-6 L+4=0 \\ L=\frac{6 \pm \sqrt{20}}{2}=3 \pm \sqrt{5} \end{gathered} $$ Since $a_{1}=2>0$, the limit of the increasing sequence is $L=3+\sqrt{5}$.
Q18 [10 pts] Long
series-convergence root-test comparison-test
Determine whether the given series is convergent or divergent. Give brief reason to justify your answer for full credit. (a) $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \sqrt{\frac{n}{n^{2}+1}}$ [3 pts] (b) $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} e^{-2 n} \sin n$ [3 pts] (c) $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{n}{2 n+1}\right)^{n^{2}}$ [4 pts]
Determine whether the given series is convergent or divergent. Give brief reason to justify your answer for full credit.
(a) $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \sqrt{\frac{n}{n^{2}+1}}$
[3 pts]
(b) $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} e^{-2 n} \sin n$
[3 pts]
(c) $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{n}{2 n+1}\right)^{n^{2}}$
[4 pts]
▶ Show Answer & Solution
(a) The series is divergent by the limit comparison test since $$ \lim _{n \rightarrow} \frac{\sqrt{\frac{n}{n^{2}+1}}}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}=\lim _{n \rightarrow} \sqrt{\frac{n^{2}}{n^{2}+1}}=\lim _{n \rightarrow} \sqrt{\frac{1}{1+1 / n^{2}}}=1, $$ and the $p$-series $\sum \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ is divergent. (b) Since $$ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|e^{-2 n} \sin n\right| \leq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} e^{-2 n}=\frac{e^{-2}}{1-e^{-2}}<\infty $$ the given series is absolutely convergent, hence convergence. (c) By the root test, $$ 0 \leq \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty}\left(\frac{n}{2 n+1}\right)^{n} \leq \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{1}{2^{n}}=0<1 $$ the series is convergent.

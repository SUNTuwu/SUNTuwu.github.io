Q17 [12 pts] Long
improper-integral comparison-test
Let $f(x)=\frac{\ln \left(1+e^{2 x}\right)}{e^{2 x}}$, where $0 \leq x<\infty$. (a) Using the substitution $u=e^{2 x}$ and integration by parts, or otherwise, evaluate the improper integral $\int_{0}^{\infty} f(x) d x$. (b) If the area under the graph of $f$ is rotated about the $y$-axis to generated a solid of revolution, is the volume of the solid finite? Justify your answer for full credit. ![](https://cdn.mathpix.com/cropped/9db39384-013f-491a-9c82-1c9ba2dff317-08.jpg?height=476&width=469&top_left_y=2067&top_left_x=1416)
▶ Show Answer & Solution
(a) Let $u=e^{2 x}$, such that $d u=2 e^{2 x} d x=2 u d x$. $$ \begin{gathered} \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\ln \left(1+e^{2 x}\right)}{e^{2 x}} d x=\int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{\ln (1+u)}{2 u^{2}} d u=\int_{1}^{\infty}-\frac{1}{2} \ln (1+u) d u^{-1} \\ =-\left.\frac{\ln (1+u)}{2 u}\right|_{1} ^{\infty}+\frac{1}{2} \int \frac{1}{u(u+1)} d u=\frac{\ln 2}{2}+\frac{1}{2} \int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{u}-\frac{1}{u+1}\right) d u \\ =\frac{\ln 2}{2}+\frac{1}{2} \ln \left|\frac{u}{u+1}\right|_{1}^{\infty}=\frac{\ln 2}{2}+\frac{\ln 2}{2}=\ln 2 \end{gathered} $$ (b) The volume of the solid of revolution is finite since $$ \begin{gathered} \int_{0}^{\infty} 2 \pi x \cdot \frac{\ln \left(1+e^{2 x}\right)}{e^{2 x}} d x \\ =2 \pi \int_{0}^{1} \frac{x \ln \left(1+e^{2 x}\right)}{e^{2 x}} d x+2 \pi \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{x \ln \left(1+e^{2 x}\right)}{e^{2 x}} d x \\ <2 \pi \ln \left(1+e^{2}\right)+2 \pi \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{x \ln \left(2 e^{2 x}\right)}{e^{2 x}} d x \\ <2 \pi \ln \left(1+e^{2}\right)+2 \pi \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{x(2 x+1)}{(2 x)^{4} / 4!} d x<\infty \end{gathered} $$ as $e^{2 x}=1+2 x+(2 x)^{2} / 2!+\cdots>(2 x)^{4} / 4!$ for $x \geq 0$. Or show that $\frac{4 x}{e^{x}} \frac{\ln \left(1+e^{2 x}\right)}{2 e^{x}}<\frac{4 x}{e^{x}}$.
Q18 [12 pts] Long
power-series interval-of-convergence
Determine whether the given series is convergent or divergent. Give reason to justify your answer for full credit. (a) $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2^{n}+3^{n}}{3^{n}+4^{n}}$. [4 pts] (b) $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(2 n)!}{n^{n}}$ [4 pts] (c) $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^{2}+\sin ^{2} n}$ [4 pts] 19. $[14 p t s]$ Let $f$ be defined by the power series $f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n} \frac{(x-2)^{n+2}}{(n+1) 8^{n}}$. (a) Find the 4 -th order derivative $f^{\prime \prime \prime \prime}(2)$. (b) Determine the interval of convergence of the power series. Show your steps for full credit. (c) Find the value of $f^{\prime}(4)$.
▶ Show Answer & Solution
(a) Convergent by the Comparison Test: $$ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2^{n}+3^{n}}{3^{n}+4^{n}}<\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2 \cdot 3^{n}}{4^{n}}<\infty $$ (b) Divergent by Ratio Test: $$ \begin{gathered} \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{\frac{(2(n+1))!}{(n+1)^{n+1}}}{\frac{(2 n)!}{n^{n}}}=\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{n^{n}(2 n+2)(2 n+1)}{(n+1)^{n+1}} \\ =\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{2(2 n+1)}{(1+1 / n)^{n}}=\infty>1 \end{gathered} $$ (c) Convergent since the series is absolutely convergent: $$ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^{2}+\sin ^{2} n}\right|<\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^{2}}<\infty $$
